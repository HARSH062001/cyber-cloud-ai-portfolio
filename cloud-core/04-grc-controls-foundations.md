# 04 GRC Controls Foundations

Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) helps organizations decide what must be protected, what could go wrong, what controls are needed, and how those controls are evidenced.

## Risk

Risk is the possibility that a threat will exploit a weakness and cause harm. In practice, risk discussions often focus on likelihood, impact, and business context.

## Controls

Controls are safeguards used to reduce risk. They may be technical, administrative, or procedural.

Examples include:

- MFA enforcement
- secure configuration baselines
- access reviews
- logging and monitoring
- patching requirements

## Compliance

Compliance means aligning with required standards, policies, or frameworks. Compliance alone is not the same as security, but it often drives structure, accountability, and documentation.

## Evidence

A control is stronger when there is evidence that it exists and is working. Evidence may include screenshots, logs, review records, reports, configuration outputs, or meeting records.
